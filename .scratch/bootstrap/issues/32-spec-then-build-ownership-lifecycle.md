# 32: Spec-then-build: the ownership and lifecycle invariants

**Context:** AGENTS.md states knarr's controller invariants: it writes only the annotations it owns, on live Pod objects, never changes replica counts, never touches a Deployment's pod template, and stops patching a pod once `deletionTimestamp` is set. OVERVIEW §4, §6, §7, §8 and §9.3 and §9.19 decide them, and no elicitation ticket's acceptance checks name them. [Decision 0009](../../../docs/decisions/0009-allium-objective-map.md) assigns them here, in the `ownership` and `lifecycle` modules, next to the clauses 22 and 23 elicit. Those tickets draft their own spec-then-build follow-ups; they amend this ticket rather than duplicate it. Ticket 30 adds the Event and metric each invariant emits.

**What to build:** Clauses for each decided behaviour below, written with the `tend` skill without a live session because OVERVIEW already decides them, then tests derived with `propagate` and the controller code that passes them, in the order the `gleam-change` skill sets.

**Non-goals:** Any open question 22 or 23 owns. The `deletionTimestamp` safeguard, which 31 builds with the loop.

**Blocked by:** 22, 23, 30

**From 17:** [Decision 0009](../../../docs/decisions/0009-allium-objective-map.md), map rows for §4, §6, §7, §8 and §9.

**From 30:** [observability.allium](../../../docs/specs/observability.allium) names what these invariants emit, and [ticket 30's Settled table](30-elicit-observability.md#settled) is the record. This ticket emits the rejection and yield signals: `knarr_patch_rejections_total{deployment, purpose}` when a rejection is known, `knarr_yields_total{deployment, annotation}`, and the `AnnotationYielded`, `PatchRejected` and `CleanupRejected` Events (`Events` `Yielded`, `Rejected` and `CleanupRejected`), each cause with its warning log line (`Events` `AlsoLogged`). The reconcile core ([31](31-spec-then-build-reconcile-core.md)) emits `knarr_annotations` and `knarr_targets` and builds `Cadence` `entered` and `Logs` `render`; the client ([41](41-k8s-client-spec-then-build.md)) makes each create, one attempt counted on `knarr_events_total{reason, result}`; the patcher ([46](46-spec-then-build-poll-schedule-and-patcher.md)) is the one emitter of `knarr_patches_total{deployment, purpose, result}`, counted at response time, whose results this ticket's tests assert. The boxes below name the Event and metric each invariant emits.

**From 23:** [lifecycle.allium](../../../docs/specs/lifecycle.allium) is settled, and [23's Settled table](23-elicit-cleanup-staleness-restart.md#settled) is the record. The `Cleanup` contract (`due`, `done` over `View`, `Held` and `Removal`) makes a cleanup when a pod or an annotation leaves knarr's management and at no other time, derived from each discovery cycle's state with no memory, for annotations standing owned only (`Scope`); it removes the annotation and drops its record, removing the marker when no record remains while a yielded or conflict record keeps it (`Marker`); one not accepted is forgotten and due again at the next cycle no sooner than the patch interval (`Retry`); it is a pending change with purpose `cleanup` under budget's `order` (`Order`); `done` tells observability when a Deployment's cleanup is finished (`Done`); it counts under the deployment label the View carries (`Label`); and both signatures are pure (`Pure`). The `Restart` invariants say no annotation expires by age and the reading window is the only expiry (`NoExpiry`), the marker's times decide nothing (`Informational`), a restarted knarr has no startup pass (`NoStartupStep`), a crash's leftover is a value the new process finds and nothing moves while knarr is down (`Leftovers`), and the §9.16 threshold has three parts (`Threshold`). The `Uninstall` invariants give the procedure and the manual recovery (`Procedure`, `Recovery`). ownership gained `cleanup` and `Released`, the patch a cleanup sends. This ticket builds the contracts and the tests the boxes below list; the discovery process that builds the Views and hands them to `due` is [31](31-spec-then-build-reconcile-core.md)'s and the pending set is [46](46-spec-then-build-poll-schedule-and-patcher.md)'s.

**MVP critical path:** yes. These are the safeguards the MVP promises.

A rejection becomes known at two seams in the reconciler: `send_patch`'s `PatchFailure` has `PreconditionFailed` for a 422 and `OtherFailure` for everything else, a 400 or 403 included, and `classify_kept` drops `rejected`'s verdict. 31's adversarial review also left an open question at the end of `ownership.allium`, whether every patch tests the pod's uid; it is this ticket's to settle, not one 22 owns.

- [ ] knarr writes only the annotations it owns, on live Pod objects (§4), which the `Ownership` contract states as `Adoption` and `may_write`. A yield raises ownership's Event with the reason `AnnotationYielded` (observability `Events` `Yielded`), counts on `knarr_yields_total{deployment, annotation}`, and moves the annotation to `standing="yielded"` on `knarr_annotations{deployment, annotation, standing}` (`Catalogue` `Standing`). A foreign annotation raises no Event: it shows as `knarr_annotations{standing="foreign"}` and one warning line on entry (`Cadence` `OnEntry`, `Logs` `StateLines`). A terminating pod raises no Event and counts only in `knarr_targets{kind="pods"}` (`Catalogue` `SeriesLife`).
- [ ] knarr never patches a Deployment's pod template (§4).
- [ ] Every patch touches `metadata.annotations` only (§6), which the `Ownership` contract states as `WriteMode`.
- [ ] knarr never changes a replica count; HPA or KEDA owns scaling (§7, §9.3).
- [ ] A patch rejected with 400, 403 or by an admission webhook is logged and reported with an Event and a metric, with no rapid retries, and the previous annotations stay (§8 failure modes). The metrics are `knarr_patches_total{result="rejected"}` at response time, where a 422 counts as `precondition_failed`, and `knarr_patch_rejections_total{deployment, purpose}` when the rejection is known, a 400 or 403 at once and a 422 when the next LIST classifies it (observability `Catalogue` `Patches`). The Event is one create attempt of `PatchRejected` on the Pod on the first rejection of a run; the next accepted patch ends the run, and the run is held in memory, so a restart raises it once more; a 422 classified as a rejection reports 422 (`Events` `Rejected`). The cause logs one warning line whether or not the create succeeds, and a failed create logs a second (`Events` `AlsoLogged`). A rejected cleanup raises `CleanupRejected` instead, under the same cadence in a run of its own, so it still raises after a `PatchRejected` on the same pod (one run per reason; any accepted patch ends both), with a message naming each annotation left and the `kubectl annotate pod <pod> <key>-` that removes it, or, for a marker-only cleanup that would have rewritten the marker, naming no command, and counts on `knarr_patch_rejections_total{purpose="cleanup"}` (`Events` `CleanupRejected`). A 422 is first a precondition failure and is classified by the next LIST with ownership `rejected`: true when every test of the failed patch passes against that view, so the patch was refused for its content (ownership `Rejected`). A rejected cleanup is due again at the next discovery cycle in which every LIST succeeded, no sooner than the patch interval, and nothing else is remembered (lifecycle `Retry`).
- [ ] The `Ownership` contract of [ownership.allium](../../../docs/specs/ownership.allium) is built with a test per signature and per clause (done by 31 for every signature but `cleanup`, which 23 added after; this ticket adds the rejection, foreign and cadence tests named here and reviews 31's coverage): `read` and `render` round trip and the canonical order (`Recorded`); `standing` and `may_write` for every state, unowned, owned, conflict, yielded and foreign (`Adoption`); `patch` with annotations null and with a list, its operation order, a write `may_write` forbids being dropped, a null write on an absent annotation being dropped, and the no-op patch that sends nothing (`WriteMode`); `yield`'s operations (`Yield`); `document`'s encoding; `rejected` passing and failing (`Rejected`); `event` from the pre-yield view with a value and with absent, and `message` for both (`Event`); observability's `reason`, `PatchRejected` for apply and yield and `CleanupRejected` for cleanup, and `rejected_message` for both reasons, a 422 classified as a rejection reporting 422, and a cleanup that leaves one annotation and one that leaves two (`stays` and `stay`, the removals joined by a space) (`Events` `Rejected` and `CleanupRejected`); `Cadence` `entered` for the `rejected`, `cleanup_rejected` and `foreign` entries this ticket announces: the first rejection of a run announced and a second in the same run not, a re-entry after an accepted patch announced, and every current entry announced after a restart (`OnEntry`, `Restart`), where 31 builds `entered` and both tickets use it; and `Pure` by repeated application.
- [ ] knarr runs as a single replica with no leader election; a `Recreate` upgrade leaves a short gap with no updates and nothing else (§8, §9.19). It raises no Event and no metric of its own; the new pod answers `/readyz` 503 until its first discovery cycle in which every LIST of it succeeds (observability `Probes` `Readiness`).
- [ ] While knarr is down or crashlooping it makes no updates, and the clauses state that annotations go stale (§8 failure modes; lifecycle `Restart` `Leftovers`). Neither raises an Event: a down knarr exports nothing, a crashlooping one answers `/readyz` 503 until its first discovery cycle in which every LIST of it succeeds and starts its counters at 0 on each start (`Catalogue` `SeriesLife`), and a running knarr whose loop stalls shows as a stale `knarr_last_discovery_timestamp_seconds`, the alert, since liveness never restarts on it (`Probes` `Liveness`).
- [ ] The `Cleanup` contract of [lifecycle.allium](../../../docs/specs/lifecycle.allium) is built with a test per signature and per clause: `due` for a non-target pod with an owned record (one `Removal`), with a yielded record (none), with a conflict record (none), with no marker (none) and terminating (none); a target whose Deployment removed the safe-to-evict opt-in with an owned safe-to-evict record (that one alone) and with pod-deletion-cost owned (none); a null record with an absent annotation on a non-target (one `Removal`, the marker-only patch); and both annotations owned on a non-target (two) (`Scope`); the same pod at two consecutive cycles after a rejection giving the same Removals (`Retry`); `done` true with no due views and no outstanding, false with one due view, false with one outstanding `Removal` carrying its name though no view shows its pod, and true when the only view holding owned records out of scope is terminating (`Done`); the View's `deployment` for a non-target pod from the ReplicaSet owner's name, and empty when the chain names no Deployment (`Label`); and `Pure` by repeated application.
- [ ] ownership's `cleanup` has a test per case of `Released`: the operations for one released annotation with a value (the `deletionTimestamp` test, the marker test, the value test, the remove, and the marker add without the entry and with `at` set); for a null record with an absent annotation (marker-only: the tests and the marker add); for the last entry (the marker removed); with a yielded entry kept; with an annotation not standing owned dropped; and a call left with none returning no operations (`Released`).
- [ ] The `Restart` and `Uninstall` contracts of [lifecycle.allium](../../../docs/specs/lifecycle.allium): a test or kind check that a found value is kept until the window elapses again, that nothing compares the marker's `at` or a record's `at` with a clock or another time, ownership's `read` parsing them only for usability (grep-level or a property), and that the first discovery cycle derives the cleanups due (`NoExpiry`, `Informational`, `NoStartupStep`); and `Uninstall`'s `Procedure` and `Recovery` are stated in [34](34-user-guides.md)'s operations page.
- [ ] Each clause has a test that traces to it, `just check` passes, and the hand-back gives the `plan-spec` obligation count of each module touched.
