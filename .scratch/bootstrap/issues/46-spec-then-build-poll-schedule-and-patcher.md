# 46: Spec-then-build: the poll schedule and the patcher

**Context:** Ticket [24](24-elicit-poll-budget-feature-gate.md) settled the `budget` module in `docs/specs/budget.allium`: the `PollSchedule` contract (phase, delay-only jitter, bounded concurrency, no overlap, the LIST cadence, joining and leaving) and the `WriteBudget` contract (the per-pod patch interval, the global token bucket, the pending set, the exponential 429 pause and its install-wide scope), with ten install-wide values in its `config` block. Nothing runs a timer or holds a bucket yet: the poller ([43](43-status-poller.md)) sends one poll, the reconciler ([31](31-spec-then-build-reconcile-core.md)) decides whether a change is due, and neither owns when a poll fires or whether a due patch may go now.

**What to build:** The scheduler and the patcher from the `PollSchedule` and `WriteBudget` contracts in `docs/specs/budget.allium`. The scheduler: a per-pod schedule with a random phase and delay-only jitter, the concurrency bound with an oldest-due queue, no overlapping polls of one pod, and join and leave driven by the LIST and the LIST timer. The patcher: the token bucket, the per-pod interval counted from the last attempt whatever its outcome, the pending set served oldest-due first and re-derived from the latest reading when a pod's turn comes, the exponential 429 pause with Retry-After, and the LIST 429 case. The install-wide values come from OTP application configuration, as knarr's `Listening` reads the bind address, each with its default and valid range. Tests derived with `propagate` first, then the code in the order the `gleam-change` skill sets: pure functions for every contract signature with the clock and the random draw passed in, the scheduler and the patcher as processes under a supervisor, and FFI only for the clock if one is needed, in a named `*_ffi.erl` module.

**Non-goals:** What a poll reads and the timeout's enforcement (43). Whether a change is due and its re-derivation from the latest reading (31). The patch itself, the marker and other writers (32, 41). The harness that measures the write budget (33). The metric and Event names (30).

**From 21:** the joining entries the caller builds are one per listed pod with a pod IP (reconcile `NoPodIp` in [reconcile.allium](../../../docs/specs/reconcile.allium)); a listed pod without one keeps counting in `knarr_targets` and is handed to `on_list` in `listed`, but has no schedule until a LIST shows an IP.

**From 23:** budget's `Pending` gained `purpose: apply | cleanup` ([lifecycle.allium](../../../docs/specs/lifecycle.allium) `Cleanup` `Order`): there is one pending entry per pod and purpose, so a pod due both an apply and a cleanup has two; an apply entry is discarded when a LIST first shows the pod's `deletionTimestamp` set or no longer shows the pod, where the pods the LIST shows are the target set, and a cleanup entry is kept while lifecycle's `due` names the pod, which it does only for a live pod the pod LIST returns that is not terminating, so an opt-out keeps the cleanup and drops the apply (budget `Pending`); when the pod's turn comes a cleanup is re-derived from the latest view as an apply is from the latest reading. The per-pod interval is counted across purposes, from the pod's last attempt whatever its outcome or purpose, and a cleanup of a pod that is not a target takes `config.patch_interval`, since its Deployment is not in the selected LIST and its override cannot be read (budget `PerPod`, lifecycle `Retry`). The first discovery cycle after a restart may make every cleanup due at once, all with the same `due_since`, and the bucket alone bounds them (budget `Restart`, lifecycle `Restart` `NoStartupStep`): about 100 s for 500 pods at the defaults.

**From 30:** [observability.allium](../../../docs/specs/observability.allium) names budget's six signals (`Catalogue` `Budget`, `Polls`), and [ticket 30's Settled table](30-elicit-observability.md#settled) rows 8 to 10 and 28 are the record. A 429 is counted once, as `result="throttled"` on `knarr_patches_total` or `knarr_lists_total`, never on a counter of its own; the results come from the status the client ([41](41-k8s-client-spec-then-build.md)) surfaces through `Catalogue` `patch_result` and `list_result`. `knarr_paused{scope}` and `knarr_pause_seconds{scope}` cover both pauses, scope `patch` for `BackOff` and `list` for `PauseScope`'s LIST pause. The per-Deployment series are retired only once the Deployment has left the target set and nothing for its pods is pending or in flight and its cleanup is done (lifecycle `Cleanup` `done`), so a late response is counted before the series go (`SeriesLife`), and every count starts at 0 after a restart.

**MVP critical path:** yes. The write budget is a §3 success criterion, and 33 measures it against the bucket this ticket builds.

A cleanup's outcome feeds the same hook from [47](47-discovery-cleanup-hand-off.md). The pod-identity question is settled under ownership `Precondition` with no uid test; [48](48-detect-patch-on-replacement-pod.md) compares the patch response's uid with the view's once this ticket owns the response.

**From 31:** the reconciler (`src/knarr/reconciler.gleam`) is a thin driver on the LIST timer, reconcile's `Loop` `Cycle`: one `Tick` per `config.list_interval` runs the discovery cycle, polls every target pod with an IP once through `fan_out` (linked worker processes, at most `poll_concurrency` at a time, `poller.poll` over `poller_http.send` with `poll_timeout`), and `write` and `yields` send each due patch at once through `k8s_client.patch_pod`, with no per-pod interval, bucket or pause; a 429 is a failed patch left to the next cycle. ### Choices a maintainer may reverse

- A LIST counts once on `knarr_lists_total`, not once per page (observability `Budget`).
- A crashed or overdue poll worker gives its pod a `timed_out` reading, as 31's fan-out did; the deadline is twice `poll_timeout` plus a second.
- The token is spent at the attempt, not reserved at the grant; `NothingDue` spends nothing. The plan's Decision 6 said reserve and refund; Codex's Threshold breach reversed it.
- A departing Deployment's series leave at three moments: `knarr_polls_skipped_total` at the LIST whose cycle leaves it no scheduled pod (the scheduler removes it), `knarr_patches_pending` by the patcher at `Quiet`, and every other series, gauges included, by the reconciler at `Quiet`. Letting the gauges go at the LIST is three lines in `discovered` and one assertion.
- Poll duration is wall-clock milliseconds from the worker's own clock, not a monotonic reading; no clock FFI was added.
- A LIST that changes a pod's interval after a rung was computed keeps that rung; the rungs after it climb by the new interval (budget `Jitter`).
- The ladder climbs when the rung comes due, and a rung passing while the poll is queued counts as a skip, as one passing in flight does.
- A dropped pod's in-flight worker is stopped with a `shutdown` exit, not left to finish; its response, had it come, would be discarded anyway.
- The patcher's `Listed` carries pod names and the target Deployments only, so a pending entry's interval and Deployment refresh only by a new `Due`; the reconciler sends a `Due` on every poll that finds something due, which is what makes a changed override reach a pending entry.
- Per-Deployment state gauges move at the LIST, while the Events and state lines fire at the poll that enters the state; a rebuild per `Polled` was rejected as the plan says.
- `still_due` after a yield is computed, not fixed true: a yield that leaves nothing due drops the entry rather than costing a `NothingDue` turn an interval later.
- A failed token or namespace read clears the session; until the next good read every turn is answered nothing due, readings are still counted and observed, and a state entered meanwhile is announced by the next cycle that reads the token.
- A 422 answers `still_due` false and nothing is handed over for the pod until the next LIST (reconcile `Rejected422`); the alternative, keeping the entry and refusing the turn, costs a turn per interval.
- The LIST pause guard runs before the token read, so a token rotated during the pause is seen at the first tick after it, at most `backoff_cap` later.
- During a LIST pause nothing reaches the patcher, so it keeps the previous target set: a pod deleted meanwhile keeps its apply, whose turns fail at the apiserver until the next good LIST drops it, bounded by the pause.
- The LIST timer rides a process-owned subject because `erlang:send_after` to a registered name resolves the name when the timer fires, so a killed reconciler's timer would tick its replacement and every crash would add a LIST chain; the scheduler's `Wake` and `Deadline` and the patcher's `Wake` stay on the names because each is idempotent or pid-guarded.
- Budget's `Purpose` has constructors `Apply` and `Cleanup`, which collide with observability's `Purpose`; the reconciler qualifies budget's.
- The scheduler logs a warning, `poll worker not started`, when the factory refuses a child; the poll stays in the queue and the wake is re-armed within a second to send it again.
- No new open question was recorded in any specification; the one open question in `observability.allium` (`Tiers` against reconcile `NoPodIp`) predates this ticket.

### Residual risks

- Several tests run against the real clock (the scheduler's phase, deadline, stale-wake, deadline-cancel and shutdown-report tests, the last synchronised through the factory's children, the patcher's two timer tests and its stale-wake test, the reconciler's replaced-timer, second-chain and pause-timer tests) and the negative checks elsewhere use a fixed quiet window; each can go false-green under heavy load, never false-red (the two `a_list_429_on_*` tests pause on a Retry-After of 120, so the LIST pause's own real timer cannot race their withheld-tick check).
